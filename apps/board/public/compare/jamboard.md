# Jamboard is gone. Here’s a free replacement for teachers.

> Google shut Jamboard down on 31 December 2024. Rupok is a free, open-source whiteboard for teachers: write on slides, add AI-made activities, and bring your old Jams back as PDFs.

Canonical: https://rupok.app/compare/jamboard/ · Language: en · Updated: 2026-11-23

Google stopped Jamboard editing on 1 October 2024 and wound the app down on 31 December 2024. Your old Jams became PDFs in Google Drive. Rupok is free and open source: drop those PDFs in and keep teaching on them.

## Rupok vs Jamboard at a glance

*Rupok and Jamboard compared, as of November 2026*

|  | Rupok | Jamboard |

| Runs on | Chrome, Edge or Safari on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS. Desktop app for Windows, macOS (beta) and Linux; installers not code-signed yet | ✗ Nowhere: no editing since 1 Oct 2024, and the app wound down on 31 Dec 2024[^1] |

*Jamboard no longer exists, so most rows don’t apply. The rows above are what changed.*

## Where Jamboard is stronger

Being honest about this saves you time.

- **It was simple and built into Google Workspace,** with real-time collaboration in Drive and Meet. Google’s recommended replacements (FigJam, Lucidspark and Miro)[^1] are collaborative whiteboards too. If a whole class co-editing one board is what you need today, try those: Rupok’s live class is still coming.

## Where Rupok is stronger

- **Free, with no account.** Nothing to sign up for, for you or your students.
- **Bring your Jams back.** Google converted Jams to PDFs in Drive[^3]. Download one and drop it into Rupok: every page becomes a slide you can write on.
- **Fast pen ink** on any smartboard, panel, projector or laptop with Chrome, Edge or Safari (or the desktop app), including the classroom panel that replaced your Jamboard.
- **AI Smart Objects:** live simulations and quizzes on the slide.
- **Class replay:** students replay your lesson from a small `.rpk` file.
- **Open source,** so it can’t be switched off the way Jamboard was.

## Which one should you pick?

**Pick FigJam, Lucidspark or Miro** if you mainly used Jamboard for students posting sticky notes together.

**Pick Rupok** if you used Jamboard to teach: writing on slides at the front of the class, then sharing the result.

## Bringing your Jams to Rupok

1. **Find your old Jams in Google Drive.** Google converted them to PDF files[^3]. Download the ones you want.
2. **Open the board.** Go to [rupok.app/board](https://rupok.app/board/) in Chrome, Edge or Safari, on the panel's PC or your laptop. No install, no account. Prefer an app? [Download it](https://rupok.app/download/) for Windows, Mac or Linux.
3. **Drop the PDF in.** Every page becomes a slide you can write on.
4. **Add the live parts.** Ask your AI for a simulation or quiz and drop it on a slide as a Smart Object ([how](https://rupok.app/ai-whiteboard/)).
5. **Save and share.** Save the lesson as a `.rpk` file and send it to students. They replay it at [rupok.app/open](https://rupok.app/open/).

## Rupok and Jamboard: questions

### When did Jamboard shut down?

Google announced the wind-down on 28 September 2023. Creating and editing Jams stopped on 1 October 2024, and the app wound down on 31 December 2024[^1].

### What happened to my Jams?

Google’s help centre says Jams were converted to PDFs and are available in Google Drive[^3]. You can drop those PDFs into Rupok and write on them.

### What did Google recommend instead of Jamboard?

FigJam, Lucidspark and Miro, which integrate with Google Workspace[^1].

### What can I do with my old Jamboard device?

Google says that since 1 October 2024 the device runs unlicensed: it still works as an offline whiteboard (without saving to Drive) or as an HDMI display[^2].
